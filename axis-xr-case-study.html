01 / The research problem

From device lists
to spatial attention.

In the headset recording, the same three cameras can be viewed as a conventional list or distributed across the physical environment. One is marked healthy, one warns about temperature, and one has no connection.

The design hypothesis is simple: spatial proximity, depth and visual emphasis may help people notice problematic devices sooner and identify where to inspect them. The interface is a research prototype—not a claim of validated performance.

03 / Evidence & limitations

Honest about what
the interface proves.

The original recording is being used to reconstruct the interaction, rather than publishing an identifiable lab video. The demo statuses shown here are taken from the recorded prototype and are not live VAPIX readings.

This public case study demonstrates the visualization and interaction concept. It does not establish task-time improvements, camera availability in production, validated indoor navigation, or experimental significance.

Source: Axis XR Monitoring project (ongoing course work). The complete Unity implementation, prototype evolution and research evaluation are maintained separately from this simplified web experience.
